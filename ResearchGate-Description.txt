Chess as a Strategic Structural Intelligence Laboratory (Chess-SSIL) is an open research framework for investigating how autonomous strategy generation can evolve from bounded competitive games toward broader, verifiable structural intelligence. Building on Structural Intelligence, competitive UIRTU-Relay, and Strategic Structural Intelligence, the project examines game-state localization, per-node decision intelligence, strategy generation, self-play, independent evaluation, counter-evidence, and structural growth. Its Strategy-Generability Partition (SGP) distinguishes problems suitable for reliable autonomous strategy search and learning from residual problems requiring further modeling, evidence, or oversight. Residual-to-Generable Conversion (RGC) investigates how decomposition, improved evaluators, simulation, and reusable intelligence units may progressively expand the verified generable domain. The repository includes six research papers, three interface specifications, testable hypotheses, a validation roadmap, and architecture figures. Chess-SSIL is a research proposal and collective-learning entry point; it does not claim to have demonstrated autonomous structural evolution or artificial superintelligence.
